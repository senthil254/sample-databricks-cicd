Chapter 1

🤖 Meet the robot

Imagine a robot chef in a kitchen far away.

  • It is brilliant at cooking
  • It follows recipes perfectly
  • It never gets tired 😴

But there is one catch.

🤔 The robot cannot read your handwriting. Your recipe is at your house. The robot is somewhere else. Someone has to carry it over.

Carrying recipes by hand goes wrong a lot:

  • You grab yesterday’s recipe by mistake 📄
  • You drop page two on the way 💨
  • Nobody remembers which one the robot has 🤷
Chapter 2

📚 The magic recipe book

So we got a magic book called GitHub.

  • Every recipe lives inside it
  • It remembers every single change ever made
  • You can always flip back to yesterday’s version ⏪

Then we built a delivery van that drives recipes to the robot, all by itself. 🚚

Grown-ups call the van a pipeline. Fancy word. It just means: a line of steps that happen in order, automatically.

Chapter 3

👅 The taste tester

Here is the clever bit! Before the van leaves, a taste tester checks the recipe.

  • Does 2 + 3 really make 5? ✅
  • Is anything missing? 🔍

Our taste tester checks two things every time:

what the tester says
test_add_numbers        PASSED  ✅
test_normalize_name     PASSED  ✅

2 passed in 0.01s

🛑 If even ONE check fails, the van does not move. No bad recipe ever reaches the robot. Not once.

The whole check takes about eight seconds. ⏱️

Chapter 4

🏠 Two kitchens, not one

The robot has two kitchens. This turns out to matter a lot.

KitchenWhat happens thereWho eats it
🧪 PracticeTry wild ideas. Make a mess!Just you
🍽️ RealOnly recipes that truly workEverybody

Practice kitchen: every save goes there. Mess away. 🎨

Real kitchen: you must put a sticker on the recipe first. 🏷️

the sticker
v0.1.0

💡 Why a sticker? So nobody accidentally sends dinner to everyone. Sticking a label on is something you have to mean to do.

Chapter 5

🪪 The robot’s own badge

The van needs a badge to get into the kitchen. But whose badge?

At first we used John’s badge. Then we thought harder. 🤔

  • What if John goes on holiday? 🏖️
  • What if John changes his password?
  • What if John leaves?

Dinner stops. For everyone. 😱

✅ So we made a badge for the VAN itself. The van is not a person. It never goes on holiday, never forgets its password, never leaves.

Grown-ups call this a service principal. It just means a robot’s own badge.

Chapter 6

😅 Oops! Three mistakes

It did not work first time. It almost never does! Here is what went wrong.

🕳️ Oops 1: The invisible password

  • A helper robot wrote the password down
  • But it wrote… nothing. An empty page!
  • The list said “password: ✓” — so it looked fine

It only broke when the van actually tried the door. 🚪

💡 A password on a list is not the same as a password that works. You have to try the door.

👻 Oops 2: The invisible extra character

  • Next try, the password was written correctly…
  • …with one invisible extra Enter at the end ⏎
  • Like writing your name then a secret blank line

The door said no. And the error message lied — it said “no password given”, when really the password was there and just slightly wrong. 😤

🏚️ Oops 3: The right key, the wrong house

  • Someone made a badge — for the wrong kitchen
  • A perfectly good key… to somebody else’s door

🔍 Always check which house you are standing in before trusting the key in your hand.

Chapter 7

🌟 The big lessons

LessonWhy it matters
1️⃣Check before you sendThe taste tester catches mistakes while they are still cheap
2️⃣Practice away from the real thingMess up safely, where nobody else notices
3️⃣Looks right ≠ is rightThe empty password looked perfect on the list
4️⃣Error messages can be wrongOurs blamed the wrong thing entirely
5️⃣Robots need their own badgeSo nothing breaks when a person is away

🍳 One more! Sending the recipe over is free. Actually cooking costs money — the ovens use power. So we deliver often, and cook only when we really want dinner.

🤔 Think about it

  • Why does the taste tester check before the van leaves, not after?
  • Why give the van its own badge instead of borrowing John’s?
  • The password list said ✓ but the password was empty. How would you catch that?
  • Why should “send dinner to everyone” be harder than “try something in practice”?
